Security

Encrypted on the way up, plain files on your disk.

Both halves are the promise: the copy in the cloud is unreadable to us, and the copy on your machine is yours to open, read and keep.

End-to-end encrypted sync
Notes are sealed on your Mac before they are sent. The service stores ciphertext it cannot read — the key never leaves your machine.
PBKDF2-HMAC-SHA256 and AES-256-GCM
A 256-bit key is derived from your password with PBKDF2-HMAC-SHA256, using the account's salt and iteration count. Notes are sealed with AES-256-GCM. No hand-waving: those are the exact primitives, and this audience checks.
Your password and key stay with you
The server never receives either one. Sign-in proves knowledge of the password through a separate verifier derived from the key, so the service can confirm who you are without being able to decrypt anything.
What the cloud can see
For each note the service sees its id — the document key — and its last-updated time, which decides last-writer-wins. The stored envelope also carries the version and the key-derivation parameters needed to open it. Title, body, tags and attachments stay ciphertext.
On your disk: ordinary Markdown
The local library stays plain Markdown files. Encryption protects the sync path, not your disk — so you can still open any note in another editor, grep it or commit it to git.
Two places to keep the cloud copy
Cloud storage through your Drafta account is the default and is what the plan covers. Connect your own CouchDB instead and it stores with no quota — locally or on a server you control.

Local first

Your notes are files, not rows in someone's database.

Drafta keeps the library on your disk: one Markdown file per note, YAML front-matter for tags and status, revisions in a folder beside it. No closed format stands between you and your own writing, and changing plan cannot strand it. The cloud copy is encrypted under a key you hold, not one the service holds — so it stays yours even when it is not on your disk.

Because it is plain text, the rest of your toolchain already works: grep across everything, commit the library to git, open a note in VS Code or Obsidian, or cat it in a terminal. Drafta is a native Swift app — there is no Electron runtime underneath and no bundled browser to keep hot.

  • Plain Markdown with YAML front-matter on your disk
  • 30 revisions per note, stored locally
  • No proprietary database, no export-only escape hatch
  • Version control, grep and any other editor keep working
  • Licensed under Apache-2.0

FAQ

Questions worth answering

What does Drafta require?

macOS 26.4 or later, on Apple Silicon or Intel. It is a native Swift and SwiftUI app, not a web app and not an Electron shell.

How do I install it?

Download the DMG from the latest release, open it and drag Drafta.app into Applications. The build is not notarised yet, so the first launch needs right-click → Open → Open; Gatekeeper asks only once.

How do updates work?

They arrive by themselves. Sparkle checks drafta.org/appcast.xml once a day, and Drafta → Check for Updates… does it on demand.

Is there a trial, and does it need a card?

Every new account gets 30 days. No card is required to start it. When it ends you pick a billing period — monthly or yearly.

Where do my notes live?

On your disk, one Markdown file per note with YAML front-matter, revisions in a folder beside them. Nothing about the storage format is proprietary — if you stop using Drafta, your notes still open anywhere.

Do I need an account to use the app?

An account is what starts your trial, carries your plan and — unless you connect your own CouchDB — gives your notes a cloud copy. That copy is stored as ciphertext we cannot read.

What does a plan include?

There is one plan, and you choose how to pay for it: monthly or yearly. The card above shows the storage and note limits read from the accounts API, so this page never quotes a quota the service does not enforce.

Is my data encrypted?

On the way to the cloud, yes — end to end. Notes are sealed with AES-256-GCM under a 256-bit key derived from your password with PBKDF2-HMAC-SHA256, using the account's salt and iteration count. Be precise about the boundary: this protects the sync path, not your disk. The library on your Mac stays ordinary Markdown files you can read in any editor.

Can you read my notes?

Not the synced copy. The server never receives your password or your key — sign-in proves knowledge of the password through a separate verifier derived from the key — and what it stores is ciphertext under a key it does not hold. For each note the id and the last-updated timestamp are visible, along with the envelope's version and key-derivation parameters; the timestamp is what decides last-writer-wins between your Macs.

Where is my data stored?

In two places, and both are yours. Locally: plain Markdown files in your library folder, plus revisions. In the cloud: storage through your Drafta account is the default and is what the plan's quota covers. If you would rather not use it, connect your own CouchDB instead — storage with no quota, locally or on your own server.

What if I use a PC or Linux?

Not yet. macOS is available today; Windows and Linux are planned on Rust and Tauri. It is on the roadmap below rather than in the app.
