Security

Encrypted on the way up, plain files on your disk.

Both halves are the promise: the copy in the cloud is unreadable to us, and the copy on your machine is yours to open, read and keep.

Unreadable in the cloud. Ordinary Markdown on your Mac. The same promise, kept twice
AES-256-GCM notes sealed on your Mac before they are sent
2 passwords: one signs you in, the library one never leaves your Mac
End-to-end encrypted sync
Notes are sealed with AES-256-GCM on your Mac before they are sent, under a key that comes from your library password through PBKDF2-HMAC-SHA256. The service stores ciphertext and never holds the key. No hand-waving: those are the exact primitives, and this audience checks.
Two passwords, two jobs
Your account password signs you in: it goes to the server over TLS, like any sign-in. Your library password is separate, never leaves your Mac and is what the encryption key comes from. So the server can tell it is you, but it cannot decrypt your notes.
What the cloud can see
Your account email and plan, and for each note its id and last-updated time — the time decides last-writer-wins between your Macs — plus the size of the encrypted payload. Title, body, tags and attachments stay ciphertext.
On your disk: ordinary Markdown
The library on your Mac stays plain Markdown files. Encryption protects the sync path, not your disk — grep, git and Obsidian keep working, and FileVault is what protects the disk itself.
A library password we cannot reset
The server never had it, so nobody can recover it for you. Your notes on this Mac stay readable files either way; the encrypted cloud copy opens only with that password.
Two places to keep the cloud copy
Cloud storage through your Drafta account is the default and is what the plan covers. Connect your own CouchDB instead and it stores with no quota — locally or on a server you control.

Local first

Your notes are files, not rows in someone’s database.

Drafta keeps the library on your disk: one Markdown file per note, YAML front-matter for tags and status, revisions in a folder beside it. No closed format stands between you and your own writing, and changing plan cannot strand it. The cloud copy is encrypted under a key you hold, not one the service holds — so it stays yours even when it is not on your disk.

Because it is plain text, the rest of your toolchain already works: grep across everything, commit the library to git, open a note in VS Code or Obsidian, or cat it in a terminal. Drafta is a native Swift app — there is no Electron runtime underneath and no bundled browser to keep hot.

FAQ

Questions worth answering

What does Drafta require?

macOS 26 or later, on Apple Silicon or Intel. It is a native Swift and SwiftUI app, not a web app and not an Electron shell.

How do I install it?

Download the DMG from the latest release, open it and drag Drafta.app into Applications. The build is not notarised yet, so macOS blocks the first launch: open System Settings → Privacy & Security, scroll to the message about Drafta and click Open Anyway, then confirm. macOS asks only once.

How do updates work?

They arrive by themselves. Sparkle checks drafta.org/appcast.xml once a day, and Drafta → Check for Updates… does it on demand.

Is there a trial, and does it need a card?

Every new account gets 30 days. No card is required to start it. When it ends, Drafta becomes read-only: you can open, search and read every note, while creating, editing and exporting wait for a plan — monthly or yearly. Card payment is not connected yet.

Where do my notes live?

On your disk, one Markdown file per note with YAML front-matter, revisions in a folder beside them. Nothing about the storage format is proprietary — if you stop using Drafta, your notes still open anywhere.

Do I need an account to use the app?

Yes. The account starts your trial, carries your plan and — unless you connect your own CouchDB — keeps an encrypted cloud copy of your notes. Its password only signs you in; the notes are encrypted with a separate library password that stays on your Mac.

What does a plan include?

There is one plan, and you choose how to pay for it: monthly or yearly. The card above shows the storage and note limits read from the accounts API, so this page never quotes a quota the service does not enforce.

Is my data encrypted?

On the way to the cloud, yes — end to end. Notes are sealed with AES-256-GCM on your Mac under a key that comes from your library password through PBKDF2-HMAC-SHA256. Be precise about the boundary: this protects the sync path, not your disk. The library on your Mac stays ordinary Markdown files you can read in any editor; FileVault protects the disk.

Can you read my notes?

Not the synced copy. There are two passwords: the account password is sent to the server to sign you in, and the library password never leaves your Mac — the encryption key comes from it. The server can authenticate you, but it stores only ciphertext and has no key to open it. For each note it sees the id, the last-updated time — which decides last-writer-wins between your Macs — and the size of the encrypted payload.

What if I forget my library password?

We cannot reset it: it never reached the server. The notes on your Mac stay plain Markdown files, but the encrypted cloud copy can only be opened with that password — keep it in a password manager.

Which AI providers does the assistant use?

The one you bring a key for: Anthropic (claude-haiku-4-5), OpenAI (gpt-4o-mini) or DeepSeek (deepseek-chat). Keys are stored in the macOS Keychain.

What can the MCP server do?

It ships inside the app and gives Claude and other MCP clients 12 tools over your whole library: search and read notes, list notebooks and tags, read the formatting guide, and create, append to, update, tag, re-status and trash notes, or create notebooks. Rewrites keep the previous text as a revision. Want an assistant that only looks? Run the server read-only and the write tools disappear.

Where is my data stored?

In two places, and both are yours. Locally: plain Markdown files in your library folder, plus revisions. In the cloud: storage through your Drafta account is the default and is what the plan’s quota covers. If you would rather not use it, connect your own CouchDB instead — storage with no quota, locally or on your own server.

What if I use a PC or Linux?

Not yet. macOS is available today; Windows and Linux are planned on Rust and Tauri. It is on the roadmap below rather than in the app.
